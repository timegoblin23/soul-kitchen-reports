The net narrative

If the whole week had to go into one sentence: we are paying more for the traffic and the people arriving are buying roughly what they were buying before. CPM is up 39% against the launch week. Link CTR is down about a quarter of a point. Purchase rate per landing page view is down under a point. AOV has not moved — £25.36 on the first day, £23.94 today, and never far from £25 in between. If the creatives are leaning harder on the discount, it is not showing up in the basket yet.

The interesting movement is upstream of the purchase, and it is a reshaping rather than a collapse. The add-to-cart rate held around 36–42% through the first five days, then fell to 26% on the 27th and 21% on the 28th. Checkout completion moved the other way over the same days — 33% on the 22nd against 60% on the 28th — so the two roughly cancel and the purchase rate lands in the same place. Fewer people are carting; the ones who do are buying more decisively. That is consistent with softer, broader traffic, and it is also the least settled reading on the page: 27–29 October have had the least time to accrue attribution, so this is the first thing to re-read once the window closes.

What is keeping the account standing is weight, not quality. The bundle campaign carries about two-thirds of the spend and has the weakest cart rate of the three campaigns. The MOF/BOF campaign carries a seventh of the spend and has the best cart rate, the best checkout-to-purchase rate and the best ROAS. The account-level purchase rate looks stable at 10–11% because those two are blending; underneath, the big campaign is softening and the small one is doing more of the work than its budget suggests.

The cost of reaching people is the problem. The offer, the basket and the buying intent are not the problem yet.

The next step

The play: scale MOF/BOF

It is the cheapest traffic in the account at £10.25 CPM, it converts 17.2% of landing page views into purchases against 10.2% for the bundle campaign, and it does that on £60 a day. It is the one part of the machine that is working as intended.

The open question is not whether it is efficient today. It is what the next £60 buys. If scaling finds new top-of-funnel people at a similar cost, CPM stays near £10–11 and the cart rate holds above 45%. If it is buying the same people more often, CPM climbs and the cart rate falls. Those two numbers together are the test.

The other thing to watch in the same move: its checkout rate is already the lowest of the three at 78.6%. More cold traffic will test that first.
