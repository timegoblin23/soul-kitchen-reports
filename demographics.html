Female wins on efficiency, not just volume.

Female is 84.9% of spend and 86.2% of revenue, with 2.38x ROAS against 2.14x for male. Female CPA is £9.57 versus £11.10, and female link CTR is 1.91% versus 1.62%.

Cheap impressions are not cheap purchases.

Male CPM is £12.38 versus £14.71 for female — male is cheaper to reach. But male CPA is £11.10 versus £9.57, and male ROAS is 2.14x versus 2.38x. Male AOV is higher (£23.70 vs £22.79), but not enough to close the gap.

65+ is the efficiency engine.

65+ is 30.7% of spend and 34.0% of revenue, with 2.60x ROAS, £9.01 CPA, 2.76% link CTR and £0.65 link CPC — the best on all four. It also has the highest CPM (£17.91).

55-64 is the volume centre.

55-64 is 34.7% of spend and 35.4% of revenue, the largest age band. ROAS is 2.39x and CPA £9.59 — nearly as efficient as 65+, with almost £65k of spend behind it.

CPM rises with age; CPA falls.

65+ pays £17.91 CPM versus £9.09 for 25-34, yet its CPA is £9.01 versus £10.44 and its link CPC is £0.65 versus £0.73. High CPM can still mean low CPC and low CPA when CTR is high enough.

25-34 is the odd one out.

25-34 has the lowest ROAS (1.88x) and the lowest AOV (£19.65), despite a mid-pack CPA of £10.44. The lower basket value — not just the cost to convert — is what drags its ROAS.

04 · Campaign mix check

Is the skew just the campaign?

The two campaigns delivered at different times. The older-female skew is present in both, and the September relaunch is even more concentrated — so this is structural, not a one-campaign artifact.

The skew strengthened in the relaunch.

45+ was 88.0% of the 2025 campaign's spend and 96.0% of the September 2026 relaunch. 65+ moved from 29.2% to 47.1%. The relaunch did not broaden the audience; it concentrated it further.

The efficiency jump is broad-based.

The 2026 campaign's ROAS is 6.9x–8.2x in every age band, versus 1.65x–1.96x for the 2025 campaign. The demographic mix explains part of the audience, but the performance lift is not a demographic artifact.

05 · What the data says

Six readings.

These are descriptive findings from the demographic breakdown, not causal claims. The campaign mix check is the main control.

1. Female-first, and more efficient.

Female is 84.9% of spend and 86.2% of revenue, with 2.38x ROAS and £9.57 CPA. Male is cheaper to reach but more expensive to convert.

2. 45+ is the account.

45+ is 88.6% of spend and 90.5% of revenue. 18-34 is 2.7% of spend. This is not a broad all-age account.

3. 65+ is the efficiency engine.

65+ is 30.7% of spend and 34.0% of revenue, with the best ROAS (2.60x), CPA (£9.01), link CTR (2.76%) and link CPC (£0.65).

4. 55-64 is the volume centre.

55-64 is 34.7% of spend and 35.4% of revenue, with 2.39x ROAS and £9.59 CPA. It is the largest and second-most efficient band.

5. CPM and CPA move in opposite directions.

65+ pays £17.91 CPM versus £9.09 for 25-34, but its CPA is £9.01 versus £10.44. Cheap impressions are not cheap outcomes.

6. The profile is concentrated.

The top two age × gender cells are 54.4% of spend; the top four are 82.5%. Female 45+ alone is 74.9%. The tail is thin.

06 · What this does not tell us

Read the shape, not the cause.

Demographic breakdowns describe delivery and attributed outcomes. They do not prove why a group converted, and small cells can move quickly.

Inferred demographics

Meta's age and gender breakdowns are based on account information, not a census. 1.8% of spend is unknown gender, and a small unknown-age row is an attribution residual. It is excluded from rankings.

Delivery, not targeting

These are the demographics of people reached, not the targeting settings. The account used Advantage+ broad targeting; the observed skew is the delivery outcome, not an input.

Campaign mix

The 2025 campaign and the September 2026 relaunch are blended. The campaign mix check shows the skew is present in both, but creative resonance and seasonality can still explain part of the difference.

Small cells

18-24, 25-34 male and 35-44 male are under £1,000 spend. Their rankings are directional. The heatmap marks them with *.

Attribution

Revenue is Meta-attributed purchase value. ROAS and AOV inherit the attribution window and event-matching caveats. They are not banked revenue.

Age bands

Meta's bands are 18-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55-64 and 65+. The 65+ band is open-ended. No 13-17 delivery appears in this account.
